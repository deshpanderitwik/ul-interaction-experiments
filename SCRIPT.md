# The Thesis Video — Script & Scaffold

> Working surface for the **1–3 minute thesis video**. `THESIS.md` holds the
> argument and the corpus audit; this file holds **the words and the shots**.
> Everything below is scaffolding to write into — tighten freely, nothing is
> precious. `_write:_` marks a slot waiting for prose.

## The core framing (the argument the video makes)

Five joints. Each must survive in the final script, even if compressed to a
single spoken line:

1. **The port.** When music-making went digital, we didn't design new
   instruments — we photographed the old ones. The piano became a *picture* of
   a piano; its geometry became the piano roll inside every DAW; the drum
   machine became a picture of pads; the mixer, drawings of faders. The field
   runs on portraits of hardware.
2. **Why that was reasonable.** Those interfaces were masterpieces of
   legibility, refined over centuries — and familiarity transfers. But their
   shapes were *answers to physical constraints*. A piano key is a lever
   **because a string needs a hammer**. The interface was never the point; it
   was the compromise.
3. **The medium shift** (never say "unlimited flexibility" — name it). The
   glass has no strings, so it owes nothing to the hammer. And it has three
   properties no instrument has ever had:
   - the control surface **is** the display — what you touch, what you see,
     and what sounds can be *one thing*;
   - input is **continuous** — position, motion, paths — not a lattice of
     switches;
   - the instrument can **change while you play it** — remember, accumulate,
     move on its own. A piano looks identical before and after you play it.
     A screen doesn't have to.
4. **The indictment (sharpest blade — keep).** The painted piano is doubly
   wrong: it **keeps the piano's limits** (the fixed lattice, the frozen
   layout), which no longer apply — and **loses the piano's virtues** (weight,
   travel, touch), which don't survive the port. We took the most
   reconfigurable surface ever made and froze it into a photograph of the
   least flexible thing about the old world.
5. **The turn.** The instrument native to glass hasn't been designed yet.
   **What does music-making look like when it's *born* on the screen, instead
   of ported to it?** → the demonstrations answer.

**Optional bigger frame** (cold-open candidate): every new medium begins by
imitating the old one — early cinema filmed stage plays until it discovered the
close-up and the cut. This *predicts* a native grammar exists and is findable,
and recasts the corpus as the search for music's close-up.

## Voice & register

- Short declaratives. Present tense. Spoken-word rhythm — read every line aloud.
- No jargon on screen or in VO: never "affordances," "skeuomorphism,"
  "paradigm." Say "painted it on the screen."
- The demos carry the argument; VO only *names* what the eye already saw.
  If a line explains something not visible, cut the line or change the shot.
- One question mark in the whole script (the turn). Everything else asserts.

## The spine

Act I poses the question; Act II answers it as four claims — each claim is one
property of the born-on-glass instrument, demonstrated by sketches that exist
today; Act III resolves and points onward.

| Beat | Claim | Sketch(es) | ~Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| I · The Port | (setup + question) | archive footage / painted keys | 20s |
| II·1 | Touch, image, and sound are one thing | duet / raindrops | 15s |
| II·2 | A gesture doesn't trigger — it leaves a living trace | bodies / extended grid | 25s |
| II·3 | The traces are instruments themselves | bent paths / path explorations | 30s |
| — bridge | …and the whole field is material too | extended scroll + mode flip, drums enter | 10s |
| II·4 | A composition is a place, not a timeline | rings I–IV / 3D stack / ring joining | 25s |
| III · Resolve | (hands off; restate the turn as invitation) | the piece playing itself | 15s |
| III · Coda (stretch) | the world is next | sampler waveform (needs new build) | 5–10s |

Total ≈ 2:20 with coda — inside the 1–3 window with room to trim.

## Script scaffold

### I · The Port — *the setup and the question*

- **PURPOSE:** land joints 1–5 in ≤ 25 seconds; earn the right to demo.
- **SCREEN:** _write/decide:_ options — (a) actual screenshots of painted-piano
  apps, quick montage; (b) a bare piano-keys graphic that freezes into a flat
  "screenshot" of itself; (c) black screen + type only.
- **VO (draft v1 — workshopped):**
  > When music went digital, we didn't design new instruments. We took the
  > piano — and painted it on the screen.
  > But a piano key is a lever because a string needs a hammer. The glass has
  > no strings. It can be anything —
  > and we froze it into a picture of the past.
  > So — what does an instrument look like when it's *born* on glass?
- **SOUND:** _write:_ (silence → a single dry piano note? the note that becomes
  the first ripple of II·1?)
- **NOTES:** the cinema analogy fits here if used — before "when music went
  digital." Costs ~6s. Decide in Open decisions.

### II·1 — *Touch, image, and sound are one thing*

- **PURPOSE:** the atom of the whole thesis in one second of footage. No UI, no
  structure — one touch, one event of light+sound.
- **SCREEN:** finger touches glass → ripple blooms + note sounds (raindrops /
  duet language). Two or three touches, unhurried.
- **VO:** _write:_ (candidate: "Here, what you touch is what you see — is what
  you hear.")
- **SOUND:** the sketch's own audio. First musical material of the piece.
- **NOTES:** this beat must feel like *relief* after Act I's indictment.

### II·2 — *A gesture doesn't trigger — it leaves a living trace*

- **PURPOSE:** persistence + accumulation. A scene builds in front of you.
- **SCREEN:** extended grid: plant a body — it joins the groove in time; plant
  three more; ripples layer; position=pitch reads like a staff. Drag one — the
  music changes as it moves.
- **VO:** _write:_ (must name the trace idea: the gesture *stays* and keeps
  playing. Candidate: "A gesture here doesn't trigger a note. It leaves
  something behind — alive, in time, still playing.")
- **SOUND:** the accumulating groove — this becomes the video's backing track.
- **NOTES:** the piano-contrast callback is available: *a piano looks the same
  after you play it; this doesn't.*

### II·3 — *The traces are instruments themselves* (the emotional core)

- **PURPOSE:** the deepest claim — you can reach *into* committed music and
  play it as material. Longest beat; earns the runtime.
- **SCREEN:** lay a path → body travels it (melody = motion). Then: drag a
  waypoint mid-flight; **pluck** a segment — it bows and twangs; flip a node
  🎲 (it wanders each cycle); give another ✏️ a sub-path orbit. The piece
  starts developing itself.
- **VO:** _write:_ (candidates to place: "the melody is a drawing you can
  grab" / "pluck the music itself" / "and then it starts to improvise.")
- **SOUND:** the path's arp over the II·2 groove; the twang must be *heard*
  clearly once, dry.
- **NOTES:** slow down here. One gesture per breath. The pluck is the
  money-shot of the whole video.

### — bridge — *…and the whole field is material too*

- **PURPOSE:** the instrument's *ground* moves, not just its figures.
- **SCREEN:** scroll the window — everything re-registers; flip the mode —
  same motion, new harmony; drums fade in underneath (combinations).
- **VO:** _write:_ (one line max, or none.)
- **NOTES:** candidate to cut first if over time. Mode-flip-as-gesture is a
  pre-shoot build (see THESIS.md gaps).

### II·4 — *A composition is a place, not a timeline*

- **PURPOSE:** structure without a piano roll — the frontier claim.
- **SCREEN:** rings: a pattern as a circle; layers stack in colour; tilt into
  the 3D stack — the arrangement as an object you look at; ring joining —
  sections as tokens in a column, zoom into one, out, switch.
- **VO:** _write:_ (must land "no timeline anywhere." Candidate: "There's no
  timeline here. The composition is a place — and you're standing in it.")
- **SOUND:** the rings' own polymeter; consider phase-locking key with the
  II·2 groove for continuity (see Open decisions).
- **NOTES:** the tilt-to-3D move is the most cinematic frame in the corpus.

### III · Resolve — *hands off*

- **PURPOSE:** proof of "instrument, not toy": the accumulated piece stands on
  its own. Restate the turn as an invitation, not a conclusion.
- **SCREEN:** hands leave the glass; the composition plays; slow pull-back or
  slow zoom on the living scene.
- **VO (draft):**
  > None of this existed before the glass. All of it was waiting there.
  > _write: final line — the invitation._
- **SOUND:** the full piece, then a controlled decay or hard cut to black on a
  downbeat.

### III · Coda (stretch — needs the new native build)

- **SCREEN:** the phone lifts; the **sampler** waveform breathes with the room's
  sound.
- **VO:** _write:_ (candidate: "And it's just starting to listen.")
- **NOTES:** ship only if the TestFlight build lands + sampler participates
  musically. Otherwise end on III·Resolve.
