01

Distribution-first changes the definition of done

The problem to solve: a maker-first habit of building things to a point where they're "not that shareable."

The fix: a sketch isn't done when it works — it's done when there's a clip posted to X. The build is the input; the post is the deliverable. "Not shareable" stops being a valid terminal state.

Sharing your work increases your opportunity surface area. — Emil Kowalski
02

The niche Locked

It evolved across the conversation: haptics → experimental/spectacle → finally:

Touch-native experimental interfaces as art — genuinely beautiful, novel interactions that show what a phone can do when pushed, at the intersection of interaction design and art direction.
  • Not about haptics or a TestFlight funnel.
  • Not the crowded craft/polish lane (Puckett, Kowalski, Freiberg, Coursey).
  • Not the cold dev-first WebGL lane (DesLauriers, Awwwards/Codrops crowd).

The wedge

The spectacle world is mostly developer-first (great tech, weak art direction, demos not interfaces) and web/desktop. Almost nobody does technically ambitious spectacle that's genuinely interaction-designed AND art-directed, on mobile, driven by touch. That intersection is open.

03

Platform decision: mobile-native Locked

Chosen deliberately over web/WebGL. Constraints turned into edges:

  • Video-only → vertical video is the native X format; cross-posts free to Reels / TikTok / Shorts.
  • The phone is the subject, not the constraint → the viewer watches on the exact device the work is for. "My thumb could be doing that."
  • Touch is the moat → multitouch, pressure, momentum — what a mouse (the whole WebGL crowd) structurally can't show.

Honest costs

No live "playable link" (the video does 100% of the work → production value is mandatory); lower top-end vs desktop GPU; you must become excellent at capture. Note: Skia's SkSL shaders + Reanimated mean the mobile ceiling is higher than people assume.

04

Competitive analysis

Case study: Josh Puckett's launch post for "Means & Methods" (Interface Craft).

  • A successful launch post is a harvest, not a seed — it converts years of compounded attention. No trick in the tweet.
  • Ferocious positioning wins. Everything rolls up to one ownable flag: "uncommon care."
  • The medium is the portfolio — the product demonstrates the skill it sells; no gap between claim and proof.
  • The peer graph is the real "surface area" — a cluster of high-craft people amplifying each other.

What you can't copy: his two decades + pedigree + compounded audience + paid product. Don't fight head-on — take the adjacent, uncrowded mobile/touch lane.

05

What you're really selling

A personal brand is a company of one. Apple→taste, Stripe→care, Disney→nostalgia. The rules:

  • Sell the feeling, not the thing. The "thing" is Skia interactions; the brand is the feeling they leave.
  • Skip your own marketing words. "Experimental / novel / stunning" are self-narration — the surface. Drop them.
  • Plain words can be deepest (Stripe sells care, not "craft").
  • Having a trait isn't selling it. Technical skill is a trait, not the brand.
  • The truth test = where your effort actually goes. The brand is only true if revealed behavior matches it.
  • A too-hard decision is a clue you haven't resolved what you sell.

Name the payer's feeling — there are two buyers

The scroller (free, drives reach) feels wonder + desire — "why isn't my phone like this?" This is Disney's magic: manufacturable, makes the clip travel.

The opportunity-giver (founder / studio / recruiter — the real payer) feels trust in rare taste at the frontier — "this person can see our product's future and make it feel inevitable." This is Disney's nostalgia: the moat, can't be faked, compounds.

→ Build the brand around the payer's feeling; use wonder as the distribution engine.

06

The flag + thesis Working

Flag: "Interfaces that don't exist yet." Plain, not a self-marketing adjective, makes you lean in — the feeling lives underneath.

Thesis line: "a future you can feel."

Disney sells nostalgia — longing backward. This sells the forward version: longing for an interface that should exist but doesn't. Same moat shape; the ache is the product.

Alternatives considered: "Futures you can feel," "Make-believe interfaces."

07

What you actually obsess over

Self-reported truth test: a mix of how it feels to use and whether the idea is new.

→ You sell the intersection: feel × novelty. Neither alone works (feel-only = polish; novelty-only = cold demo).

The leak, named

You did not pick care/finishing or virtuosity. The unloved finishing 10% — sound, the perfect loop, the grade, the cut — is exactly what converts a felt new idea into a stunning, shareable artifact, and exactly what the maker-first self skips. Importing finishing-discipline is the key habit to build.

08

Visual vocabulary — "film stock" To refine

Direction: "Warm computational" — the future as warm / physical / alive, cutting against both cold sci-fi neon and grey corporate SaaS. Rules that make the feed recognizable with the handle covered:

  • One stage: warm near-black (#0E0E0C); a paper-white variant for "daylight" pieces.
  • One accent per piece, from a tight curated palette — restraint = recognizability.
  • A signature texture: subtle film grain / noise on every piece (the texture you're reading on right now).
  • One physics personality: everything spring-based, nothing linear; touch tracks sub-frame.
  • One idea per frame: full-bleed, vertical, generous negative space, wow inside 1s.
  • Sound as a first-class layer — soft, organic, musical, never "UI beep." (This is the care leg you skip → it's a rule, not a preference.)
09

Content pillars

  1. Impossible primitives — a familiar control (slider, toggle, keyboard) behaving like nothing you've seen but feeling inevitable. Best reach-to-effort ratio → open here.
  2. Living matter — interfaces made of simulated material (fluid, light, cloth, magnetism) shaped by touch.
  3. Gestural grammar — genuinely new interaction models that only exist because of multitouch. Most defensible.
10

First flagship piece Planned

A slider that isn't a slider — value as a living substance. You push/pull a volume of luminous elastic matter; it tracks finger velocity, overshoots, sloshes, and settles with a wobble and a single satisfying tone.

Familiar (legible in 1s) × new (it's matter, not a handle) × feel-forward (velocity / overshoot / sound) × touch-native (the moat) × scoped (actually finishable). Doubles as a stress test of the whole visual vocabulary.

11

Cadence & format

Flagship-driven, not daily. Spectacle is higher ceiling / higher variance / higher effort. Reputation compounds on the best 3–4 pieces, not on consistency. One jaw-dropper > thirty tidy ones.

Format spec for X

  • Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, H.264/AAC, up to 60fps (motion is the point — never 30). ≤512MB / ≤140s.
  • Never crop to horizontal: vertical gets the Immersive Media Viewer (full-screen, sound-on) — the best stage for wonder. Horizontal letterboxes on mobile and kills it.
  • Safe zone: keep key action centered; keep essentials out of the top/bottom ~12%.

Positioning-as-art disarms the "you could never ship that" critique — claim the art frame explicitly; have a thesis, not just eye candy.

12

Capture / distribution pipeline

  • An in-app recorder was deemed overkill. Chosen: a "hide chrome" affordance + the iOS Control Center recorder (vertical video → Photos → post to X).
  • Shipped (OTA): an eye toggle in the sketch top bar — tap to hide header + status bar; double-tap top-right to restore; back button is just the chevron. Reusable across every sketch.
  • Start today, zero build: iOS Control Center recording captures full-res with in-app audio + grain, straight to Photos.
  • Capture quality is part of the product (no playable link to fall back on): 60fps, sound design, color/grade, perfect loops.
